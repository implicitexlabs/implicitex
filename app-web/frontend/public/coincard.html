Stop sharing raw wallet addresses.

Share a Coin Card.

A Coin Card is a verified USDC payment surface. It shows who you're paying, what network and token to use, and the card's current status — before any money moves.

Instead of copying a 42-character hex string and hoping it landed correctly, a sender opens a Coin Card link, sees the verified recipient and network, reviews the fee, and proceeds to the transfer surface. The card checks itself against the registry before use.

Raw wallet address

Pasting a wallet address directly works — but it carries real friction and risk:

  • 42-character string — easy to mistype, truncate, or paste wrong
  • No recipient context — who is this address?
  • No network indicator — Polygon, Ethereum, Base, or somewhere else?
  • No token indicator — USDC, MATIC, or something unexpected?
  • No status — is this address still current, deprecated, or wrong?
  • No proof layer — nothing to verify after the fact
  • No professional surface — awkward to share or publish

Coin Card

A Coin Card structures the payment identity so the sender knows exactly what they're doing:

  • Shows verified recipient name — confirmed against the registry
  • Indicates the correct network and token before transfer
  • Displays card status — active, revoked, or deprecated
  • Hands sender into the ImplicitEx transfer surface
  • Produces a receipt with the on-chain transaction hash
  • Shareable as a clean, professional link
  • Can be revoked or updated without changing the published link

A Coin Card does not replace the blockchain. It reduces the confusion before money moves — so both the sender and recipient know exactly what happened and can prove it.

Who it's for

Coin Card is designed for anyone who receives USDC and wants a cleaner, more trustworthy surface than posting a raw wallet address.

  • Creators — publish a verified payment card for support, commissions, and sponsorships
  • Freelancers — share a professional payment link on invoices and proposals instead of a raw address
  • Agencies — receive client USDC payments with verified recipient confirmation and receipt proof
  • Local businesses — accept direct USDC payments with a shareable, revocable card link
  • Donation and support pages — let contributors verify the destination before sending

You do not need to be a crypto engineer to use Coin Card. You need a wallet address and a reason someone should trust it.

How it works

When someone receives a Coin Card link:

  1. Open the Coin Card — the card checks its registry status automatically
  2. See the verified recipient, network, and token before entering any amount
  3. Enter the transfer amount in USDC
  4. Review the platform fee and total debit before any wallet action
  5. Connect your wallet and approve the exact transfer amount
  6. Confirm and execute the transfer on-chain through the ImplicitEx contract
  7. Receive a local receipt with the transaction hash — independent proof of what happened

Transfer execution is currently paused while we document our exact-approval flow for a third-party wallet security review. The Coin Card identity and verification surface is fully live. Transfers will re-enable after the review resolves. See the FAQ for details.

Non-custodial by design

Coin Card is issued by ImplicitEx and operates on the same principles as the ImplicitEx transfer platform:

  • Non-custodial — ImplicitEx never holds your funds at any point
  • No sender account required — only a compatible wallet
  • Fee shown before transfer, not after
  • Card status is checked against the registry before the transfer surface opens
  • Transfers confirmed on-chain cannot be reversed
  • The receipt includes the on-chain transaction hash as independently verifiable proof
  • A revoked card shows its revocation status and does not allow transfer handoff
